Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Wesley Chapel

A roof leak rarely starts where the stain appears. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. Often the attic has been damp for some time before any brown mark reaches the room below. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. Should the leak still be active, our crew can cover that section with a tarp for now, or time our work with the roofing contractor you pick so nothing gets sealed over an open leak.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsTurn off the breaker for any light, fan or outlet that has water running through or near it, then keep your hands off it.
  2. Contain the WaterFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneDo not climb onto a wet roof or crawl through a dark attic. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
Our Process

Our Roof Leak Water Damage Process

Moisture meters and a thermal camera let us follow the rain through the attic, the ceilings and the walls, so spots that look dry from underneath are not missed.

When rain can still get in, a temporary tarp from us or a call to your roofer comes first. We also cover floors and contents under the damaged ceiling.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Aging, cracked seals around a skylight frame that no longer keep water out.
  • Missing or broken shingles, often after high wind, with bare underlayment beneath.
  • Roof valleys where worn metal or piled debris lets water get under the covering.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • A vent pipe boot whose rubber has split from long sun exposure.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Drywall Ceilings

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Mold in the Attic

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.

Rafters and Decking

Repeated wetting makes decking, trusses and rafters turn dark, swell up and go soft. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Inside the Walls

Rain sometimes follows the framing into a wall, where the insulation and the hidden side of the drywall take it up. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Leaks blamed on age, wear or skipped maintenance are usually denied, and the roof repair itself may be handled differently from the interior. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.
