Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Wesley Chapel, Wesley Chapel Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
