Attic Water Damage: What Wesley Chapel Homeowners Should Know

Water reaches an attic from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Wesley Chapel inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Rust, water marks or drips on ductwork and metal straps in the attic
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water
  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Ceiling Collapse

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Mold on Sheathing

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. Before anyone looks, it may already have spread over a large section.

Wet Wiring

Attics hold junction boxes, recessed light housings and cable runs. Water in or on them is a shock and fire risk, so stay out until the power is checked.

Lost Insulation Value

Compressed, damp insulation does very little insulating, and it may not recover after it dries. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Our Attic Water Damage Process

Inspect and Find the Source

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

How the Damage Progresses

The first storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.
