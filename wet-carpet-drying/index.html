Wet Carpet Drying: What Wesley Chapel Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. In Wesley Chapel, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

What It Looks Like

  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • A carpet corner where the backing comes apart from the face when pulled up
What We Do

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Soaked Pad Underneath

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Stains From Furniture

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Swollen Subfloor

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Past Saving

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.
