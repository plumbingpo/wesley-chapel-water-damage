Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Wesley Chapel Restoration know it well. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Why Professional Structural Drying Matters

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. A commercial LGR dehumidifier, short for low-grain refrigerant, pulls 10 to 20 times as much moisture out of the air as a residential one, while high-velocity air movers set up the exact airflow that speeds evaporation off structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running
  • Hardwood floors that are cupping, crowning or showing gaps between boards
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
